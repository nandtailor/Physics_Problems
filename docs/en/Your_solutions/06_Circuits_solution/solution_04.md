### **The Core Concept**

When a measurement has a percentage of uncertainty (or error), it means the actual value could be slightly higher or lower than the displayed reading. To find the exact range, we need to calculate that specific percentage of the given reading and then add and subtract it from the original value.

---

### **Step 1: Given Data**

First, let's identify the values provided in the problem:

* **Measured Speed ($v$):** **60 km/h**
* **Percentage Uncertainty:** **5%**

---

### **Step 2: Calculate the Absolute Uncertainty**

We need to find out exactly how much **5%** of **60 km/h** is in actual speed (this is called the absolute uncertainty).

To do this, we multiply the speed by the percentage (converted to a decimal):

* Absolute Uncertainty = **5% of 60**
* Absolute Uncertainty = **0.05 × 60**
* Absolute Uncertainty = **3 km/h**

This means the speedometer's reading could be off by exactly **3 km/h** in either direction.

---

### **Step 3: Determine the Range**

Now, we calculate the minimum and maximum possible speeds by applying the absolute uncertainty to our original reading:

* **Minimum Actual Speed:** 60 - 3 = **57 km/h**
* **Maximum Actual Speed:** 60 + 3 = **63 km/h**

---

### **Final Answer**

Accounting for the 5% uncertainty, the range of the car's actual speed is between **57 km/h and 63 km/h**.
