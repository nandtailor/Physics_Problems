Electric current is defined as the rate of flow of electric charge. Simply put, it is the amount of charge transferred divided by the time it takes to transfer it.

### **Step 1: Given Data & Unit Conversion**

Before calculating, we must ensure all values are in standard SI units.

* **Charge ($Q$):** $30\text{ Coulombs}$
* **Time ($t$):** $2\text{ milliseconds}$

**Crucial Conversion:** We need to convert milliseconds into seconds. Since 1 second = 1000 milliseconds, we divide by 1000:


$$t = \frac{2}{1000} = 0.002\text{ seconds}$$

### **Step 2: The Formula**

The mathematical formula for average current ($I$) is:


$$I = \frac{Q}{t}$$


*(Current = Charge / Time)*

### **Step 3: The Calculation**

Now, substitute the given values into the formula:


$$I = \frac{30}{0.002}$$

To make the division easier, multiply both the numerator and the denominator by 1000 to remove the decimal:


$$I = \frac{30000}{2}$$

$$I = 15,000\text{ A}$$

### **Final Answer**

The average current of the lightning bolt is **$15,000\text{ Amperes}$** (or $15\text{ kA}$).
