### **Topic: Determining the Absolute Uncertainty of a Digital Reading**

**1. Given Data:**

* **Measured Value (T):** **25.4°C**
* **Condition:** The absolute uncertainty is exactly half (1/2) the value of the last recorded digit.

---

**2. Step 1: Identify the Value of the Last Digit (Resolution)**

* Look closely at the thermometer reading: **25.4°C**.
* The last digit ('4') is located in the first decimal place (the tenths place).
* This indicates that the device measures in increments of **0.1°C** (e.g., 25.3°C, 25.4°C, 25.5°C).
* Therefore, the value of the last digit (also known as the instrument's resolution) is **0.1°C**.

---

**3. Step 2: Calculate the Absolute Uncertainty**

* According to the given rule, to find the absolute uncertainty, we must divide the value of the last digit by 2.
* **Formula:**

$$\text{Absolute Uncertainty} = \frac{\text{Value of the Last Digit}}{2}$$


* **Calculation:**

$$\text{Absolute Uncertainty} = \frac{0.1}{2} = 0.05$$



---

**4. Final Answer & Conclusion:**

* The final absolute uncertainty of this measurement is **±0.05°C**.
* **Physical Interpretation:** This means that even though the digital screen displays **25.4°C**, the true temperature actually lies somewhere within the continuous range of **25.35°C** to **25.45°C**.
