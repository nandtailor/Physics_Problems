### **The Core Concept**

When a measurement is recorded with a plus-minus ($\pm$) value, the main number is the **measured value**, and the $\pm$ number is the **absolute uncertainty** (the margin of error). To find the percentage uncertainty, we simply need to calculate what percentage the error is of the total measured value.

---

### **Step 1: Identify the Given Data**

From the recorded measurement $t = 5.45 \pm 0.22$ seconds, we can extract:

* **Measured Value:** **5.45 seconds**
* **Absolute Uncertainty:** **0.22 seconds**

---

### **Step 2: The Formula**

The mathematical formula to calculate percentage uncertainty is:


$$\text{Percentage Uncertainty} = \left( \frac{\text{Absolute Uncertainty}}{\text{Measured Value}} \right) \times 100$$

---

### **Step 3: The Calculation**

Now, substitute the given values into the formula:


$$\text{Percentage Uncertainty} = \left( \frac{0.22}{5.45} \right) \times 100$$

To make the division easier, you can eliminate the decimals by multiplying the numerator and denominator by 100:


$$\text{Percentage Uncertainty} = \left( \frac{22}{545} \right) \times 100$$

First, divide 22 by 545:


$$\frac{22}{545} \approx 0.04036$$

Next, multiply by 100 (which simply moves the decimal point two places to the right):


$$0.04036 \times 100 = 4.036\%$$

---

### **Final Answer**

Rounding to two decimal places, the percentage uncertainty of the time measurement is **4.04%**.
